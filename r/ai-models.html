The AI Model Market

Who earns what across frontier, open-weight, coding, media, search and vertical AI models, what tokens cost, and how far revenue trails the capital going in.

01

Where the money lands

Menlo Ventures puts enterprise spending on generative AI at $37B in 2025, up from $11.5B in 2024 [1]. The split is close to even: $19B on applications and $18B on infrastructure, of which $12.5B went to foundation model APIs [1]. Inside applications, coding is the largest single department at $4.0B [1].

Enterprise generative AI spend, 2025

Source: Menlo Ventures (Dec 2025) [1]. Model APIs alone are a third of all enterprise AI spend.

02

Frontier labs: two companies, most of the revenue

OpenAI and Anthropic have both grown run-rate revenue several times over within 2026. Anthropic went from about $9B at the end of 2025 to $47B in May and above $65B by the end of July [5][6]. OpenAI was reported near $70B annualized in late September, up from about $21B at the end of 2025 [2][3]. Run-rate multiplies a recent month by twelve, so it runs ahead of booked revenue: OpenAI's own plan shows $36B of revenue for calendar 2026 [7].

03

Who enterprises pay for model APIs

In enterprise API spend, the lead has changed hands. OpenAI held 50% in 2023; by late 2025 Anthropic had 40%, OpenAI 27% and Google 21% [1]. The top three together take 88% of enterprise API dollars [1].

Enterprise LLM API share by spend

Source: Menlo Ventures survey of enterprise buyers [1]. Shares for 2023 sum to less than 100% in the source; the remainder is shown as other.

04

Open-weight models win tokens, not dollars

Open-weight models are cheap to run and dominate some usage counts. On OpenRouter, Chinese open-weight models reached about 61% of tokens by May 2026 [8]. In enterprise budgets the picture reverses: open-source models fell from 19% of enterprise API usage in 2024 to 11% in 2025, and Chinese open-source models were about 1% [1]. Volume follows price; contracts follow the frontier.

Open-weight share: usage vs enterprise spend (%)

Sources: Wing VC analysis of OpenRouter data (Jun 2026) [8]; Menlo Ventures (Dec 2025) [1]. OpenRouter skews toward developers and price-sensitive traffic, so it is not a market-wide measure.

05

Coding is the first breakout use

Enterprise coding spend grew from $550M in 2024 to $4.0B in 2025, and Anthropic models held 54% of the coding market [1]. The products on top of the models are growing just as fast. Cursor passed $4B in annualized revenue in June 2026, double its February level [9], and Anthropic said Claude Code was above $2.5B in February [4].

Coding: product run-rates and model share

Sources: Dealroom [9], Anthropic [4], Menlo Ventures [1]. Cursor runs largely on models from the labs, so some of its revenue is also lab revenue.

06

Image, video and voice: hundreds of millions

Media models earn hundreds of millions, not tens of billions. Voice leader ElevenLabs reached about $500M ARR in spring 2026 and raised at an $11B valuation [10]. Midjourney is estimated at $500M for 2025 [13]. Runway is estimated at $200M ARR in August 2026 [11], and Black Forest Labs, which licenses its image models to Meta, Adobe and Canva, at $96M in mid-2025 [12]. Google and OpenAI do not break out revenue for Veo, Imagen or Sora.

Media model companies

Sources: Sacra [10][11][12], Latka [13]. All revenue figures here are third-party estimates.

07

Search, retrieval and vertical models

AI search and enterprise retrieval sit in the same range as media. Perplexity reported about $450M ARR in March 2026 [15]; Cohere, which sells models and embeddings to enterprises, ended 2025 at $240M ARR [14]. Vertical tools are where the next tier of spend sits: Menlo counts $1.5B in healthcare AI (with $600M on ambient scribes) and $650M in legal for 2025 [1]. Harvey is estimated at $300M ARR in May 2026 [16].

08

Price per token: falling fast, except at the top

For a fixed level of capability, prices collapse. Epoch AI found the cost of GPT-4-level performance on PhD-level science questions fell about 40x per year, with a range of 9x to 900x across benchmarks [18]. A GPT-4-level MMLU score cost $37.50 per million tokens in March 2023 and $0.18 with Gemini 2.0 Flash in February 2025 [18]. Flagship list prices tell a different story: they dropped through 2025, then new premium tiers pushed the top back up [19][20].

09

Capital in, revenue out

The money going in still dwarfs the money coming back. AI startups raised $211B in 2025 [21], and global venture hit $510B in the first half of 2026 alone, with $217B going to OpenAI and Anthropic [22]. Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Microsoft plan roughly $725B of 2026 capex, up from about $410B in 2025 [23]. OpenAI's own forecast has it spending about $856B on compute through 2030 and burning $278B of cash on the way [7]; Bain estimates the industry needs $2T in yearly revenue by 2030 to fund compute and will be about $800B short [24].

Spending vs revenue ($B)

Sources: Crunchbase [21][22], FT tally via AI Weekly [23], Reuters/FT via The Star [7], Bain [24]. Run-rates and capex are not the same kind of number; the comparison shows scale, not a profit and loss.

The two largest labs now earn real money, roughly $135B of combined run-rate. That is still under a fifth of what four cloud companies plan to spend on infrastructure this year, and both labs remain cash-negative.
