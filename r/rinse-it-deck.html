Rinse It in 12 Slides

A wearable mask that rinses the face and neck without drips, wet sleeves or splashed counters.

Use ← → keys or swipe

01 · Problem

Rinsing at the sink is messy

  • Water runs down the arms and into sleeves
  • The hairline and neck get wet
  • Small basins, 16 to 20 inches, mean splashed counters [1]
  • Some people say the mess is why they skip their routine [1]

02 · Frequency

74%

of women surveyed have both a morning and an evening skincare routine, so the mess can happen twice a day [2]

03 · Today's fixes

Headbands and wristbands only absorb

  • Spa headbands keep hair dry; wristbands catch drips [3]
  • DIY: sweatbands, cut-up socks, washing in the shower
  • None of them rinse the face. You still splash by hand

04 · Alternatives

What people pay today, top of range

US$. Notes' competitor analysis and retailer listings [3][4].

05 · Concept

A face and neck mask that rinses for you

  • Shaped like an LED therapy mask, extended over the neck
  • Small clean-water reservoir and pump spray an even rinse
  • Runoff goes to the sink, not down the arms
  • Rinse upright, hands-free; washable antimicrobial liners

06 · Market

$34.5B

global beauty device market in 2024, forecast to reach $108B by 2034 at 12.1% a year [5]

07 · Adjacent markets

Forecast size, US$ billions

Forecast years 2032 to 2034 [5][6][7]. Estimates differ by publisher.

08 · Buyers

Three personas

  • Busy professional, 30 to 45: hates water on shirts, short on time
  • Eco-conscious millennial, 25 to 40: wants reusable, low-waste tools
  • Gen X self-care enthusiast, 45 to 60: buys spa-like devices, dislikes bending over
  • Men are a second wave: only 40% of U.S. men keep a routine [8]

09 · Pricing

Two tiers, between headbands and LED masks

Proposed basic US$50 to 70, premium smart model US$150 to 200. Top of each range shown. Not yet tested with buyers.

10 · Next

What has to be proven

  • How many people find the mess a real problem (survey)
  • Whether they will wear a full mask to rinse (prototype)
  • Purchase intent at several price points
  • Hygiene, weight and cosmetic-only claims to stay outside medical rules
